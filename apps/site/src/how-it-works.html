How it works

Your password never leaves the browser

Signing in proves who you are without sending the secret that proves it. Here is what happens, step by step, and what ends up where.

You type your master password

Your browser stretches it with 600,000 rounds of PBKDF2 into a master key. That pause before the vault opens is the same pause an attacker pays for every guess.

On your device

Two keys split off

One key unlocks your vault and stays put. A second, separate key — one that cannot be worked back to the first — is what the server sees to confirm it is you.

On your device

Only ciphertext is stored

Entries are sealed before upload. The server keeps encrypted blobs and a hash of the login key. A full breach of it yields nothing readable.

On the server
What the server holds

Built so a breach is boring

What the server stores

  • Encrypted entries it holds no key for
  • A hash of your login key — not of your password
  • Your vault key, locked with keys only you can derive
  • Your email as a keyed hash, not in plain text

What it never sees

  • Your master password
  • Your recovery key
  • The names of the sites you use
  • Any password, note, or code in your vault
One key in the middle

Why changing your password is instant

Your entries are not encrypted with your password. They are encrypted with a random vault key, created once when you sign up. Your password only locks that one key.

  • Change your master password and only the vault key is re-locked — your entries are untouched
  • A second copy of the vault key is locked with your recovery key, which is how recovery works
  • Every entry is sealed with AES-256-GCM and a fresh nonce, and bound to its own ID

See the full key hierarchy →

Master password │ PBKDF2 · 600,000 rounds ▼ Master key ├─► Key-encryption key │ └─► unlocks the Vault key │ └─► encrypts your entries │ └─► Login key ─► sent to the server (checked, never usable for decryption)

There is no password reset. That is the point.

Because the server cannot read your vault, it cannot let you back in either. When you sign up you get a recovery key, shown once and never sent anywhere. It is the only other way in. Keep it somewhere safe and offline.

Lose both the master password and the recovery key, and nobody — including whoever runs the server — can open the vault.
